PAGES OPENED

  • The use of warm orange lighting and why? Roger Deakins. Las Vegas is red, against the cold of Los Angeles. Wallace’s interior is lit as if by sun. The look makes no reference to the first film.
  • Blade Runner 2049 Roger Deakins. Wallace is golden and clean, from direct lamps. Vegas is far more red, through a Tiffen deep-amber filter made for the shoot.
  • Uncanny Valley: Blade Runner 2049 American Cinematographer. Lee 790 Moroccan Pink and Lee 105 Orange on the lens for that red. Green-gelled light reading yellow through the filter. Golden Amber higher up, less heavy.
  • Denis Villeneuve’s most difficult challenges IndieWire. A silver/white film instead of the black of the original. A yellow thread that becomes red in Vegas, the edges still yellow.
  • Blade Runner: Cronenweth’s Photography American Cinematographer. High contrast and shafts of light, after Citizen Kane. Crowds kept colorless. Neon for the street, dimmed just above a flicker.
  • Blade Runner title sequence Fonts In Use. Theatrical titles in Goudy Oldstyle. The workprint used Impact, then Helvetica and Goudy.
  • “Duchamp is my Lawyer” 032c. Helvetica, and quotation marks. The diagonal lines, from Mies van der Rohe and the Bauhaus.
  • Virgil Abloh: The Vogue Interview British Vogue, 2018. Branding in Helvetica, in quotes. He called it dry humour, dry irony.
  • “What The World Needs Is TV” British Vogue, 2 February 2021. Imaginary TV. Slow and cumbersome, against bite-sized video.
  • Industrial belt by Off-White MVC Magazine. The autumn/winter 2016 belt, extra long, yellow and black, after the yellow caution ribbon marked Caution.
